    distribution. The enumeration applies the authorization predicate alone, with none of the

    behavior — the field's job is to distinguish "found nothing here" from "searched everywhere I
    can see and found nothing" — but a reader who expects a hit distribution will file a bug against
    correct behavior. Say what `scopes_truncated` means too: the scope enumeration hit its bounded
    ceiling, so the list may be incomplete.

    `limit`. The total becomes an exact count across every readable scope rather than one scope, and
    on the Connect lane an unset limit means "all" — a caller flipping cross-spine on an existing

    The section itself is short: `search_memory` and `list_memory` accept `cross_spine` (bool) to
    span every scope the caller can read; it ignores any supplied `scope`; the response reports
    `searched_scopes` and `scopes_truncated`, which name the scopes searched under your

    The subsection is the load-bearing half and is why this task exists. Cross-spine is an opt-in
    widening, and the failure mode of an opt-in widening is an agent setting it on every call. Say
    plainly that the default is scope-confined and should stay that way for ordinary work: a
    session-start bootstrap, a recall about the repo you are in, or anything where the two-tier
    spine/overlay scope you already know is the right scope. Reach for cross-spine only when the
    thing you are looking for might live somewhere you are not — a decision made in another repo, a
    convention that spans projects, a memory whose scope you genuinely do not know. Note the two
    costs: a broader result set dilutes ranking, so a cross-spine search can rank a distant match
    above a local one; and a cross-spine call adds a second bounded scan of the readable set to
    enumerate the scopes.
