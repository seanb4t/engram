## Wave structure: why all seven plans are strictly serial

Cross-AI review raised this twice (cycle 1 finding #32, cycle 2 Agreed Concern 8): every plan in the
phase declares `depends_on` on its immediate predecessor, seven waves deep, and no plan said why. It
is deliberate, it is accepted, and this is where it is recorded so `/gsd-execute-phase` and any later
reader find a decision rather than an oversight.

**The reason is shared-file contention, not logical dependency.** GSD's wave rule is that same-wave
plans must have ZERO `files_modified` overlap, because parallel executors would otherwise write the
same file concurrently. Measured across the seven plans:

| File | Written by |
|---|---|
| `internal/store/spine.go` | 03-01, 03-03, 03-04, 03-05, 03-06, 03-07 |
| `internal/surfaces/toolclass.go` | 03-01, 03-04, 03-05, 03-06, 03-07 |
| `cmd/engram/testdata/help.golden`, `catalog.golden` | 03-01, 03-02, 03-03, 03-04, 03-05, 03-06, 03-07 |
| `docs-site/src/content/docs/guides/cli.md` | 03-02, 03-03, 03-04, 03-05, 03-06, 03-07 |
| `internal/surfaces/rules.go` | 03-03, 03-04, 03-07 |

The two pinned goldens alone are touched by all seven: every plan that adds a command, a leaf or a
flag regenerates them, and two executors regenerating the same golden in parallel produce a
guaranteed conflict on an artifact whose whole purpose is byte-exactness. `internal/store/spine.go`
is written by six of seven by design — plan 03-01 establishes it as the phase's single store-side
home precisely so there is ONE paginated iterator rather than six copies, and that architectural
choice buys correctness at the cost of parallelism.

**03-04 and 03-05 are the two plans a reviewer would re-wave first, and they still cannot move.**
Neither is logically dependent on 03-03's destructive checkpoint — `verify` and `consolidate` are
read-only. But both write `internal/store/spine.go`, `internal/surfaces/toolclass.go`, both goldens
and `cli.md`, which 03-03 also writes; they overlap each other on all four as well. Moving them into
a shared wave with anything, including each other, violates the zero-overlap rule.

**Accepted cost.** Seven serial waves at low-confidence estimates totalling roughly 674k tokens is
wall-clock expensive and carries no correctness risk. The alternative — splitting `spine.go` into six
per-plan files to buy parallelism — would reintroduce exactly the copy-pasted-sweep failure mode that
cycle 1's HIGH 4/5 were about. We take the wall clock.
