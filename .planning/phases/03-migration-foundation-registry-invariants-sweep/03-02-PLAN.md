Make the leaf package's guarantees checkable instead of merely written down: prove the four
construction-time panics, prove the seal holds against an out-of-package implementor by observing a
BUILD FAILURE, prove `Validate`'s three rules fire independently, prove the package is a stdlib-only
leaf with a scan that cannot pass vacuously, and prove the per-version decoder door is open and
currently unclaimed.

Purpose: SC3 says "nobody thought about reversibility" must not be a representable state. This plan is
the compile-time half of D-01's split — declaration PRESENCE made unrepresentable rather than merely
tested, with the behavioral half (additive-only compliance) left to plan 03-03's fixture table. The
sealed interface buys less than it appears to — it stops a foreign TYPE, not a literal `nil`, which Go
assigns to any interface-typed parameter regardless of sealing. The explicit `NewStep(rev == nil)`
panic is therefore the load-bearing line, and this plan is where it stops being an unexercised claim.
Two of the four RED cycles here exist specifically because a gate this repo has shipped before turned
out to be a no-op (durable records `x6v6qxqd6f`, `bsbsvn4hbc`).

Output: four test files, one three-line interface, a `.go.txt` build probe, and four committed,
reviewer-reproducible RED patches — plus the AST gate that turns PA-1's Phase 4 obligation from a doc
comment into a test.
